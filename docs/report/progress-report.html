2The problem, and how we are solving it

Filter life in a domestic RO is estimated, never measured — days and litres converted to a percentage. The only real sensors, TDS in and out, cannot see the water path: TDS reads conductivity, so it cannot detect a housing refitted without its cartridge, a bypassed membrane, or a loaded pre-filter — and WHO sets no health-based TDS guideline, so "TDS 12" is not a safety statement. India's service chain makes these failure modes routine: ~37.7 M waterborne-disease cases a year, AMC-dependent service visits, and documented counterfeit-cartridge incidents.

Our solution: a pressure tap on both sides of every element in the water path — including the booster pump. At steady flow, ΔP ÷ Q is a fingerprint of each cartridge, recorded when new and re-checked at every service: a loaded stage climbs (≈3× baseline), a removed or bypassed stage collapses (<0.35×), and the pump's measured boost gives a motor-health signal no domestic purifier carries today. Any water-path fault locks the dispense valve — detection that ends in protection, not just a warning light.

5Changes since the abstract

  • Track and area of exploration: unchanged. Same problem, same solution class — measured per-stage filter health.
  • Revised target claim (additions, not pivots): the abstract's four pressure sensors became five taps with the pump between stages — the pump is where a domestic RO actually raises pressure, and the tap pair around it yields a pump-health signal at the cost of one sensor. The instrumented path is now feed → sediment → carbon → booster pump → membrane → product → storage tank, matching a real domestic RO rather than a laboratory chain.
  • Added a fail-locked dispense interlock to the safety set (abstract proposed detection only): on any water-path fault the dispense valve refuses to open. Reason: Round 2's brief asks for the status of a safety mechanism — a detection that cannot act was the wrong target claim.
  • Budget revised: ₹⟦9,000–12,000⟧ → ₹13,200 (pump, solenoids, tank and the fifth transducer). No change to timeline; the ten-day bench plan in the abstract is on schedule — hardware ordering completed 25 Oct, four-state test planned 30 Oct.
